# NEXUS: design quality and repeat-use review

Scope: authenticated Student Today, Faculty home, Timetable and classroom moves. Use fictional data for engineering and usability rehearsal. This is not a review of every route, the public guide demo, or evidence that colleagues have adopted NEXUS.

## Honest design assessment

- **Art direction:** The warm canvas, forestry green and serif titles are recognizable. Keep them. The opening screen showed generic copy, a decorative ring and multiple ways of saying “next,” rather than answering distinct questions. Brand typography still uses local fallbacks; its exact render varies by device.
- **Student under pressure:** One “best move” formerly chose a class over an upcoming assignment. Keep next class and due coursework visible independently, with the authoritative room and timestamp. Surface confirmed room changes before both; retain them in focus mode. Separate finished work from open work.
- **Faculty on a phone:** Existing counts and lists are useful for investigation but bury frequently used entry points. Put today's timetable, evaluation when pending, and assignment creation in the opening section. Do not call attendance available before an eligible class ends.
- **Timetable:** Default to one day. The seven-day option means a rolling window, not Monday–Sunday. Room-change preview should read like a schedule action, show the old and proposed rooms and who is affected, and say which notifications it does not send.
- **Motion:** Expand and press feedback should explain state. Keep information immediately available without motion. Do not assume native view transitions are supported on every browser. No ambient loops, global page fades, sound, or exaggerated effects in daily workflows.
- **Not fixed or validated here:** genuine-device Android/iOS performance, assistive technology, styling and workflow consistency across other roles, institutional sign-in and record authority, account menu discoverability, dense faculty queues, cancellation/correction feedback, and slow-connection loss/recovery. No universal accessibility, performance or “world-class” claim.

Reference points: [Linear’s 2026 design refresh](https://linear.app/now/behind-the-latest-design-refresh) for predictable controls and visual hierarchy; [Apple motion guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion) and [Material motion](https://m3.material.io/styles/motion) for feedback rather than decoration; [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) for focus, reflow, contrast and controls. They are reference points, not proof of parity.

## Is it useful enough to miss?

**Hypothesis:** People will return voluntarily when checking NEXUS takes less effort than reconciling group messages, paper and spreadsheets, and the school backs its accuracy. An urgent room move is a trust test, but too rare by itself to create repeat use. Pair it with recurring jobs: next class and due work for students; upcoming sessions and pending evaluation for faculty. Adoption is not logins after announcements, mandated check-ins, reminder clicks or time spent in the app.

1. **Uncoached rehearsal:** With fictional scenarios, ask 5 students and 3 faculty to use their own phones. Do not mention UI labels or coach them. Student tasks: identify the next class time and room, confirm a room change, and open the closest due assignment. Faculty tasks: get to today’s scheduled class, find the next item to grade and find assignment creation. Capture correct first answer, wrong turns, taps, completion time and whether the participant reaches for another tool. Proposed gate: at least four of five students get next class and room right in 10 seconds or less; every participant in the room-change task gets the location correct; at least two of three faculty reach the right workflow in two actions or fewer. These are targets, not results.
2. **SIT-approved small pilot:** Only after authorization, roster-gated sign-in, an owner for the timetable and a correction/fallback process. Establish how participants do each task today. With roughly 10–20 students and 2–3 faculty, test over two normal teaching weeks. Count unprompted repeat users across distinct teaching days (report counts and denominators); compare the median time for the same jobs to baseline; record duplicate checks, stale records and disagreements. Suggested go/no-go: at least 60% of active participants choose NEXUS unprompted on three separate teaching days in week two, median effort beats baseline, no unresolved wrong-room or privacy incidents, and named staff can correct a schedule error within the agreed window. Small pilot results are directional, not generalizable.
3. **Would they miss it?** Privately ask, “If NEXUS was unavailable next week, what would you go back to?” and “What did you still double-check elsewhere this week?” Look for examples, not compliments. Keep official fallback and record export. Never manufacture dependency by removing alternate access.

Do not log names, screens containing records, or identifiable behavior in the rehearsal scorecard. Do not add reminders, real analytics or ask real users to join until the institution approves the scope. Use a tally by role and task.
